# Managing Workload and Job Demands

The job demand-control-support model is one of the most replicated frameworks in occupational
health: strain risk rises when demands are high and either control (autonomy over how and when
work gets done) or support (from managers and peers) is low. High demand alone, paired with high
control and good support, is much better tolerated - which is consistent with why a temporary
spike during a known release or launch period is not automatically concerning, while the same
spike without any clear cause or end point is worth flagging.

Meeting load is a specific, measurable form of demand: time in meetings directly displaces time
available for focused work and for breaks, and a sustained increase in meeting hours relative to
total work hours ("meeting load") is one of the more actionable signals available, because
protecting a focus block is a concrete, schedule-level fix rather than requiring any change to
the underlying workload.
