# Recovery and Rest Breaks

Occupational health research consistently finds that short, frequent breaks preserve
performance and reduce fatigue accumulation better than working straight through and taking one
long break at the end. Micro-breaks of a few minutes every hour, and at least one longer break
away from screens during the day, are associated with better sustained attention and lower
end-of-day fatigue.

"Break debt" - a gradual decline in break frequency relative to a person's own normal pattern -
tends to precede more visible strain by days or weeks, which is why it is a useful early signal
rather than only a lagging one. Recovery is also cumulative: a single low-break day is
unremarkable, but a multi-day decline in both break frequency and the gap between active work
sessions is a meaningful change worth naming to the person experiencing it, framed as a
observation rather than a judgment.
